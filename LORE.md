# SPOILS — the world bible (v1, 2026-08-01)

Canon for everything the game will become. Written with the user; grows as
milestones land. Everything lowercase in-game.

## the hard rules (these shape every other line in this document)

1. **EVERY ENEMY IS A HUMAN BEING.** There are no robots, no drones, no
   monsters, no infected — not now, not later. Nothing non-human ever
   attacked this city and nothing non-human lives in it. When this
   document says "machines" it means INDUSTRIAL PLANT — looms, presses,
   furnaces, conveyors: the dead equipment inside the mills. The only
   things that will ever shoot at you are people: strays, wardens,
   hounds, the tollman's crew. (User call, permanent.)
2. **No rarity color tiers on loot** — value is scarcity, condition and
   weight instead.
3. **The true cause of the collapse is never stated outright.** The
   mystery is the tone. Three people give three answers; none is
   confirmed in-game.
4. **Every district is a FIXED, learnable place.** transit is one
   permanent layout — the same streets every raid, forever — because
   quests send you to real addresses and raiders are supposed to know
   the ground. Weather, time of day, loot and people change; the map
   never does. (User call 2026-08-01.)

## 1. how it all started

Six years ago the city broke in the space of one autumn week. The official
line — the one printed on the evacuation notices people still find in
drawers — called it "a contamination event of undetermined origin." The
street never bought it. Ask three people in the den and you get three
answers: something got out of a lab under the spires; the water went bad
first and the riots did the rest; the government needed one big city to
fail so the others would obey. mara, who was on shift that night, says
only this: "the phones died first. everything else was people."

What is not in dispute: on the seventh night, the army stopped evacuating
and started building. They called it containment. The city called it the
cordon. Nobody official has said the city's name since — as if the word
itself were quarantined. People just say "inside."

## 2. the wire

The cordon is a ring of lattice fence, floodlight towers, and patience,
run by the **wardens** — a force that answers to nobody the street has
ever met. Their one rule has never changed: *nobody crosses. nothing
comes out.* They hold the buffer beyond the barricades with marksmen who
fire warning volleys exactly once. The bodies in the weeds past the line
are the people who believed the wire had gaps in its attention.

The wire was built in nine days from prefab lattice panel — the same
panel, mile after mile, patched and doubled over the years. Raiders judge
each stretch by its repairs: fresh weld means warden attention; rust
means a sleepy sector; plastic sheeting means a crossing the wardens
pretend not to see, for a price (see: the tollman).

What the wardens are FOR is the deepest argument in the den. Keeping
something in? Keeping everyone out of the evidence? Or just a budget
line nobody in the capital has the courage to cancel? sela knows. sela
doesn't talk.

## 3. the districts (the maps)

One sealed city, peeled open district by district. Each map is its own
WORLD — new structure, new movement, new dangers, never a reskin. On
mara's board, the tags read: transit (pulled — tonight's job), the mills,
harbor, old ward, and one tag scratched too deep to read (the spires —
nobody says that name either).

### transit (map one — live)
- **what it was:** the transit authority's district. rail yards, bus
  depots, the drain mains, workers' housing, supply warehouses.
- **why it's first:** nearest the wire, and mara ran its control room —
  she knows every valve, junction and dead camera by heart.
- **structure:** streets, houses, warehouses, vehicles, and BENEATH it
  the tunnel system — brick storm mains from a century ago, utility
  crawls, and the passages raiders sledged between them (see 4).
- **the ground itself (fixed forever — hard rule 4).** North: two
  housing blocks, the courtyard with its dry fountain, and the supply
  warehouses. The middle belt is industry, strung along the rail line
  that cuts the district east to west: the bus depot, the scrapyard
  with its crane and its own hall, the trainyard with the sidings.
  South: the school over its playground, the gallery where somebody
  still paints, the comms relay in the trees, and the autumn grove in
  the south-east corner where the oaks turned and never turned back.
  Roads run four by four; the barricade ring closes it all in.
- **the safehouse.** Tucked into the NORTH-EAST corner inside the wire
  (this said "near the south wire"; it was moved 2026-08-02 because the
  southern search kept dropping the spawn inside the playground), one
  squat house behind a
  lattice fence with a parking pad: magpie's bolt-hole, and where every
  raid begins. The drain run in the film comes up two streets from its
  door. mara pretends not to know about it. kettle definitely does.
- **hazard signature:** the wardens' overwatch on the ring, car alarms,
  and the strays who got here before you.

### the mills (map two — planned)
- **what it was:** the river industry: flour, steel, textile — one
  continuous body of buildings grown together over a century.
- **why it's rich:** the plant ran unmanned for months after the sealing
  — looms, presses and furnaces grinding through their last work orders
  with nobody on shift. whole order-books of product piled up inside
  before the power finally died. legend says one hall is still warm.
  (industrial equipment, standing dead. it is not alive, it does not
  fight — see hard rule 1.)
- **structure (nothing like transit):** ONE colossal interior. No
  streets, no houses, no sky for most of it — mill halls the size of
  city blocks, conveyor bridges, catwalk lattices over open floors,
  furnace rooms, silo clusters, freight elevators jammed between
  levels. Movement is hall-to-hall through the plant, not block-to-
  block down a road. Extraction through the coal gate or the river flume.
- **hazard signature:** the hounds den here (see 6). No horizon, short
  sightlines, sound carries down the steel. Your flashlight is a flare.

### harbor (map three — planned)
- **what it was:** the port. cranes, container fields, warehouses on
  pilings, and the evacuation fleet that never sailed — boarding started
  the night the order changed, and the ships still ride at anchor, full.
- **why it's rich:** ten thousand containers nobody ever opened, and the
  ships — floating warehouses with staterooms.
- **structure (nothing like transit):** WATER is the map. You cannot
  swim in it (six years of harbor water — nobody swims). Piers and
  breakwaters are the roads; container stacks form slot-canyons that
  reshuffle every raid; gangways and mooring lines are the only doors
  onto the ships; a rowboat or two are the crossings between quays —
  slow, exposed, unforgettable.
- **hazard signature:** warden patrol boats sweep the basin with lamps.
  Open sightlines down every canal. Gulls flush off a stack when
  something moves — the whole harbor's alarm system, free of charge.

### old ward (map four — planned)
- **what it was:** the historic heart. narrow stone streets from before
  cars, guild houses, the cathedral spire, money. When the cordon
  closed, the people of old ward barricaded THEMSELVES in — private
  security, private walls, private hoards. It did not save them.
- **why it's rich:** what the rich keep: safes, cellars, collections.
  kettle grew up a pawnbroker's son here. He pays double for anything
  with an old ward maker's mark, and never says why.
- **structure (nothing like transit):** a warren. Alleys too tight for
  vehicles, courtyard mazes behind arched gates, and the ROOFTOP ROAD —
  six years of raiders have planked the roofs together into a second
  city above the first. Streets are fast and lethal; roofs are slow and
  safe(ish); interiors connect through party walls sledged open. No
  grid, no straight lines anywhere.
- **hazard signature:** the tollman's people hold the gates and bridges
  (see 6). Bells — the old towers still have bells, and someone rings
  them when strangers pass.

### the spires (unnumbered — the scratched tag)
- The financial towers at the city's core. Glass, height, and whatever
  made "inside" a forbidden word. The tag on mara's board is scratched
  out because the one raider who came back from that direction did the
  scratching. End-game territory; the game's final questions live there.

## 4. how the tunnels were formed (transit's underworld)

Three ages, one system:
1. **the brick age** — storm mains laid a century ago under the transit
   district, tall enough to walk, built by people who thought drains
   deserved cathedrals' brickwork.
2. **the concrete age** — utility corridors, cable runs, shelter cellars
   from the war years, stitched near the mains but never connected —
   regulations kept them apart.
3. **the raider age** — the last six years. Crews with sledgehammers
   connected everything to everything: drain to cellar, cellar to cable
   run, cable run to the depot's underworks. mara's maps started it —
   she copied the transit authority's originals from memory in year one.
The result: a second transit district under the first. Two street
manholes and certain house cellars (behind bookshelves — a raider habit:
never break the wall where a warden informer can see the bricks) lead
down. The tunnels are DARK — flashlight country, always. The tollman
moved into a junction three winters ago and started charging.

## 5. the storms

The city sits in a river basin with hills on three sides; weather walks
in and cannot leave — it was true before the cordon and it is worse now,
or people just watch the sky more. Named by raiders:
- **rain trains** — storm cells queued nose to tail; a whole raid can
  happen inside one. Standard working weather.
- **double-strike storms** — the basin's signature lightning: two cracks
  a breath apart (the game's lightning has always done this; now you
  know it's canon).
- **black squalls** — harbor-born walls of water that kill visibility to
  arm's length. Wardens hate them. Raiders schedule around them.
- **the fog** — dawn river fog, the raider's true friend. Warden optics
  are useless in it. Every job mara plans ends the same way: *out
  before the fog lifts.*
Storm doctrine, taught to every new raider in the den: thunder is cover,
lightning is a spotlight — move on the roll, freeze on the flash.

## 6. enemies (all human — that's the rule of this world)

- **strays** — independents inside the wire: the desperate, the stranded,
  the ones who never registered anywhere. Unpredictable more than evil —
  some run, some beg, some shoot. First enemies (M3).
- **the wardens** — the cordon force. Marksmen on the line (the edge
  snipers you already know), floodlight crews, and — deeper in the
  city, later maps — patrol details in gray-green with real armor and
  radio discipline. Their gear is the best loot in the world and the
  most expensive to acquire.
- **the hounds** — a raider crew that stopped digging and started
  hunting: raiders robbing raiders, denned in the mills. They mark kills
  with a chalk dog on the nearest wall. Led by **brann** (below).
- **the tollman's gate** — not a gang so much as a tax office with
  shotguns. They hold tunnel junctions and old ward's bridges. Pay,
  detour, or push through — all three have prices.

### named enemies (the bad ones)
- **sela** — the wardens' legend on the transit stretch. The patient
  one. Raiders swear she lets you get exactly deep enough to know
  better before the first round arrives. Nobody has a face for her —
  only breath-fog on a rooftop and the double tap of her spotter's
  lamp. (She is the mythology of the game's edge snipers.)
- **brann** — leader of the hounds. Was a den regular in year two;
  kettle still keeps his unpaid tab pinned behind the scale like a
  wanted poster. Turned when his crew starved in a bad winter inside
  the mills and came out changed. Wears warden plate with warden blood
  in the straps.
- **the tollman** — no name, no face anyone agrees on, a ledger and a
  lantern at a tunnel junction. Prices are fair until they aren't. mara
  pays him. kettle won't. verne stitched him once and refuses to say a
  word about it — which for verne is a scream.

## 7. the traders — who they are, how they met

- **mara** — transit authority radio dispatcher, on shift the night the
  phones died. Ran her control room for nine days solid while the wire
  went up, calling routes for buses that stopped coming back. She walked
  out with the duty radio and the maps in her head. The den is the old
  transit maintenance depot just outside the wire — she still calls it
  "the depot" when she's tired. She runs the board, the radio, the jobs,
  the extracts. The voice in your ear.
- **kettle** — pawnbroker's son from old ward, got out the last night on
  a flatbed with three suitcases: two of stock, one of scales and
  ledgers. The name: he'll buy anything, "even the kettle." Knows the
  price of everything, tells nobody what he keeps in the third suitcase.
  He bankrolled the den in year two when mara's radio drew raiders like
  a lit window draws moths.
- **verne** — ER nurse posted to the line during the sealing, when
  families were split at the fence. When the order came to pull back,
  verne didn't. Has patched wardens, raiders, hounds and worse in the
  same night on the same table, and will again. The only person in the
  den who says "don't go."
How it became a den: year two, mara's stolen radio + kettle's money +
verne's table, in the depot mara knew would still have power. The tally
wall by the door keeps a chalk mark for every raider who went in and
didn't come out. Nobody erases it. It's most of a wall now.

## 7b. the den — home, and what it becomes

The den is this game's home base: the old transit maintenance depot
just OUTSIDE the wire, where mara's radio, kettle's money and verne's
table met in year two. You leave from here and you come back here.
It is not a menu — it is a place you stand in, with people in it.

**What it is on day one:** the job board by the door (mara's district
tags), kettle's scale and shelf, verne's table, the tally wall of
chalk marks, a stove, and one corner that is yours.

**What it becomes (the build-up loop).** The depot is huge and mostly
dark. Every wing you restore turns on another part of the game, and
the room visibly changes — lights come on, junk clears, someone moves
in. Planned wings:
- **your corner → your room:** the stash grows from a crate to
  shelving to a real wall of it. Where gear lives between raids.
- **verne's clinic:** better healing between raids, then treatments
  that persist. Restoring it costs meds you'd rather sell.
- **kettle's shop:** more stock, better prices, and eventually the
  third suitcase opens.
- **mara's radio room:** more jobs on the board, better intel before a
  raid (weather, where the wire is singing), and finally the district
  tags that unlock new maps.
- **the workbench:** repair guns and armor instead of replacing them.
- **the yard:** the depot's vehicle bay — get a truck running and you
  carry more out.
Every wing is paid for in SHINE (see 9), which is the whole point of
the loop: what you carry out becomes the place you live in.

## 7c. getting out — extraction

Getting in is easy (the wire leaks in a hundred places and the wardens
know it). **Getting OUT is the game.** You are carrying, and everything
that owns a way out knows it. Every extract in SPOILS should cost
something: time, brass, noise, or exposure.

**Canon shape:** each district has several ways out. Some always work,
some open only under conditions (the hour, the weather, a price paid),
and the good ones are watched. You choose your exit when you choose
what you're carrying.

### transit's exits — CANON (user picked 2026-08-01)

**1. the toll gate.** A warden sits a booth on the district edge, and
he is exactly the kind of man who takes that posting: rude, chatty,
and rich off people like you. Walk or drive up and he'll talk — he
will talk forever, about the gates, about the war he says he fought,
about how much he has made off raiders who thought they were clever.
Pay his fee and the barrier goes up: the wire stops caring, and you
drive out past the line while the counter runs down in green. Free to
leave, if you can afford to leave. (Diegetic proof that the cordon is
a business, not a principle — the deepest joke in §2.)

**2. the night freight.** The rail still runs one train. It sits in the
yard bigger and blacker than the dead stock around it, and it goes
when it goes. Climb aboard, and a departure clock starts whether
you're ready or not. Then it pulls, slowly, and takes you out under
the wire on rails the wardens gave up watching years ago.

**3. the lift (the green smoke).** Somebody in the warden air wing
sells rides. The den pays, you pop the marker, and a bird comes in
over the treeline with a rope. It is the fastest way out of transit
and the most expensive, and every raider who uses it knows that the
crew flying it could just as easily take the haul and go. One day one
of them will. Marked by a clearing off a dirt track, green smoke
curling out of it.

**Still unbuilt (kept for later maps / later versions):** the drain
(walk out through the storm mains — the baseline exit, lands with the
tunnels), the outfall (blocked when the rain has been running), and
the fog window (dawn fog blinds the wardens for a few minutes).

## 8. magpie — the player, and the photo

**magpie** — you. The callsign is kettle's joke that stuck: magpies
steal what shines. You came to the wire in year five with nothing but a
creased photograph: two kids on a pickup's tailgate. The other kid is
**tomas** — your brother, older by two years, who was working inside
the city the week it broke. Everyone you'd asked for five years said
the same thing: nobody comes out. Then someone in a border bar said the
word "raiders," and you went looking for the den.

How they took you in: your first crossing was alone, through a gap the
hounds had baited. verne rebuilt your shoulder. mara watched you refuse
to hand over the photo while half-conscious and decided you were either
useful or a lesson, and she'd sell tickets either way. kettle weighed
your first haul, paid honestly, and pinned a fresh job sheet to the
board without being asked. That was that. (The film "the wire" shows
your first REAL job — the drain run into transit.)

The long game (M6 quest arc): tomas's trail runs inward — a work badge
in transit, a name on a mills shift roster, a rowboat missing from
harbor, a rumor climbing toward the spires. The game's spine.

## 9. loot (M4 — the tarkov-style loop; NO rarity colors, ever)

Value is scarcity, condition and WEIGHT — a wet cardboard box of brass
is worth more than a shiny trinket, and everything shows its wear.
Categories with examples ("shine" is den slang for the good stuff):
- **brass & fuel** — ammunition (the true currency), lighter fluid,
  diesel jugs, propane bottles (our cylinders).
- **meds** — bandage rolls, clot powder, splints, verne's list items
  (insulin, heart pills — quest-adjacent, heavy conscience weight).
- **tools & parts** — wrenches, tape, fuses, radio tubes for mara,
  scale weights for kettle, gun parts, cleaning kits.
- **comforts** — coffee, cigarettes, batteries, soap. Small, light,
  always sells; the den runs on them.
- **keepsakes** — watches, rings, letters, photographs. kettle pays
  strangely well for old ward marks and never resells them.
- **warden gear** — plates, optics, radio crystals. Top shelf, and
  carrying it past the wire is its own crime.

## 10. guns (M2 starts the list; worn, mismatched, human)

No tiers — a gun is its condition, its ammo, and how much of the city
has passed through it. Working names:
- **kestrel** — 9mm service pistol, the city's most common gun. First
  gun of the game (the walk-in's table gun).
- **brick** — snub .38 revolver, never jams, never reaches.
- **boxer 12** — pump shotgun, transit house-clearing royalty.
- **coach** — sawn double, two answers and a reload.
- **stitch** — 9mm machine pistol, strays' favorite, eats brass.
- **vesper** — suppressed SMG, warden checkpoint issue; the quiet one.
- **ranger 30** — hunting bolt rifle, scoped, the buffer's whisper.
- **reed** — DMR, warden marksman issue. sela's voice. Owning one makes
  you a target of everyone.
- **karden** — battered AK-pattern rifle, mills-crew standard.
- **pike** — full warden battle rifle. So rare the den argues whether
  the third one ever existed.
- **melee** — pipe, hatchet, warden baton, kettle's letter opener (do
  not ask).

## 11. armor & wearables

- **jackets** — what everyone starts in (the raider jackets you already
  see in-game). Pockets, not protection.
- **flak vest** — old army surplus, stops fragments and optimism.
- **stray plate** — homemade: road signs, seatbelt straps. Loud, heavy,
  better than nothing, beloved.
- **warden soft kit** — patrol vest, actually good, quietly illegal.
- **warden plate & helmet** — the real thing. Wearing it confuses
  strays, enrages hounds, and makes wardens shoot FIRST.
- **rigs & packs** — chest rigs for speed, the big duffel for greed;
  carry weight vs noise vs silhouette is the whole outfit game.

## 12. lootable objects (containers — placed art first, loot in M4)

Already in the world: cabinets, bookshelves (some are DOORS — see 4),
tv stands, tables, crates, crate stacks, pallets, racks, barrels,
dumpsters, car trunks and glove boxes (every car has both ends now),
truck beds, bodies past the wire (their duffels), the den's own shelf
(look, don't touch). Coming with their maps: lockers and toolboxes
(mills), containers and ship cabins and the harbormaster's safe
(harbor), wall safes and cellar hoards and collection cases (old ward),
warden supply caches (anywhere the wire watches), tunnel stashes —
raider caches like the one by the drain ladder, some abandoned, some
merely hidden, some trapped by people who plan to come back.
